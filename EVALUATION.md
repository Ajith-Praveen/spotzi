# SpotZⁱ — Accuracy evaluation on independent public data

## Why this exists
SpotZⁱ's own demo data was generated by us, with fraud scenarios written alongside the rules, so its near-perfect scores prove little. To measure accuracy on data we did **not** create, we use two public synthetic datasets as the *background* (normal) claims, inject known fraud schemes plus legitimate look-alikes, and score the full, unchanged SpotZⁱ pipeline.

## Method
1. **Import** public synthetic claims unchanged: CMS DE-SynPUF Sample 1 (2008–2010 Medicare claims; hospitals and outpatient facilities) and the Synthea sample (clinicians, patients, organisations).
2. **Inject** labelled schemes into a few providers, each starting at a random month and continuing to the end of the data:
   - `dup` duplicate submissions · `units` unit inflation · `repeat` panels re-billed within days · `upcode` visits shifted to level 5 · `deceased` services after death
   - **`influx` — held out: no rule targets it** (a wave of new out-of-area patients, each receiving one identical bundle)
   - **benign look-alikes**: legitimate growth (new local patients with complete, realistic care episodes) — these should *not* be escalated
3. **Run** the full pipeline. Labels are used only for scoring and to train the forecast; never for detection.
4. **Score**: did each injected provider become a case? how well does each detector rank bad providers (AUC)? precision of the SIU queue? were look-alikes escalated? forecast AUC on a later, purged time window.

## What this shows
- **The fused Nexus Brain ranks injected bad providers far better than rules alone** (DE-SynPUF AUC 0.97 vs 0.85 for rules; Synthea 0.95 vs 0.71).
- **The scheme no rule targets (`influx`) is found** on both datasets.
- **The top of the queue is reliable** (precision @5 = 100% on both), which is what matters with limited investigator capacity.

## Known weaknesses (measured, not hidden)
- **Upcoding and unit inflation at institutions are caught only sometimes** (1/3 each on DE-SynPUF): low-volume facilities fall under the rules' minimum-volume guards.
- **False positives exist**: on DE-SynPUF only about a third of all opened cases are injected schemes; 2 of 4 legitimate-growth facilities were escalated (down from 4 of 4 before we made the change-point detector weigh *composition* change over raw volume). Some escalated background providers may be genuine oddities in the public data, but with no labels we count them as errors.
- **Claim-line recall is low** (~0.1–0.2): SpotZⁱ is designed to find *providers worth reviewing*, not to label every line.
- **Synthea is thin** (one clinician ≈ one patient), so benign look-alikes could not be tested there.

## Changes made because of this evaluation (general, not tuned to specific providers)
1. Duplicate rule now treats the **later-paid** submission as the duplicate (previously decided by record ID).
2. **Data-driven unit limits**: any code's daily units above its 99th percentile are flagged, not only codes on a fixed list.
3. Change-point detector weighs **who is treated and how** (service mix, out-of-area patients, paid per line) above raw volume growth.
4. Analysis windows are relative to the data, not the calendar; the pipeline handles single-family providers and non-numeric provider IDs.

## Limits of this evidence
Injected schemes are our own models of fraud. Results show SpotZⁱ detects *these* patterns against independent background data; they are **not** real-world fraud detection rates. Real labelled outcomes (e.g. the Kaggle provider-fraud set with your token, or a payer's closed SIU cases) are the next step.

*Reproduce:* `cd spotzi && python3 evaluate_public.py synthea synpuf` (results in `spotzi/data/evaluation/`).
